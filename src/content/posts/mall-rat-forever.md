I have a healthy obsession with malls.

Luckily, our local mall is thriving. The same can't be said for most other malls in America.

![JC Penney wing of the Haywood Mall in Greenville, SC](/images/mall-rat-1.jpg)

*JC Penney wing of the Haywood Mall in Greenville, SC (Photo by me)*

We all know anecdotally what happened. Shopping malls boomed in the 70s and 80s, tapered off in the 90s, started fading in the 2000s with the '08 economic collapse, and then all but disappeared after COVID hit.

Now most mall content is about dead malls or urban exploration of abandoned places. I consume plenty of it, mostly out of morbid curiosity, but also because I'm genuinely bummed about losing a cultural staple that shaped who I am.

I consider myself a Xennial. We're a micro generation born in the late '70s to early '80s who picked up technology in our late teens but had a childhood mostly free of the internet and social media. Too Gen X to be Millennial, too Millennial to be Gen X. We're a contradiction, but we also have some of the most diverse lived experiences because we straddle both worlds.

![A rare vacant storefront at Haywood Mall in Greenville, SC](/images/mall-rat-2.jpg)

*A rare vacant storefront at Haywood Mall, Greenville, SC (Photo by me)*

To us, the mall was life. Not just because we could stock up on the latest fashion, but because it was a social hub before everything moved online. The mall was social media before social media was a thing.

Going to the mall was an event when I was a kid. We went every Friday night. Ate at the food court. Hit all our favorite stores. I played on the playground. Then we were home before TGIF came on.

As a teen I got more adventurous. A group of us would meet up, or get dropped off by a parent, and spend hours walking, shopping, talking, and watching people. Neon lighting, crowds, and endless stores aimed squarely at our desire to be cool.

Shopping was a perk, but it wasn't really the point. The point was being social, observing other humans, and being out of the house. Indoor malls gave people a place to gather and just have a good time.

![Skylights in the center court at Haywood Mall in Greenville, SC](/images/mall-rat-3.jpg)

*Skylights in the center court at Haywood Mall, Greenville, SC (Photo by me)*

That's what makes the sharp decline so sad. We never replaced it with another form of in person hanging out. We flock to social media instead. I don't dislike social media, I actually love it. But as a substitute for the mall? No way. There's no comparison.

Like I said, I'm lucky to have a local mall that is not only still alive but thriving. I take my youngest there almost once a week, and it makes my heart happy that he's absorbed my love for it. I hope it stays the bustling center of our area.

If you have a thriving local mall, enjoy it. Go there. Keep it alive. Sure, we can get most things from Amazon these days. But the need to be around other humans? No Prime Day deal replaces that.
